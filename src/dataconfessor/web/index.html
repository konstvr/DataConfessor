What the tool needs from your data

    
  

  

    
One CSV with three columns. Build the manifest writes it for you by joining a
       folder of files to whatever labels CSV your dataset came with, matching on file name.


path,label,patient_id
images/ISIC_0015719.jpg,0,IP_3075186
images/ISIC_0052212.jpg,1,IP_3075186

    
path

    
Relative to the manifest, or absolute. Images (.png .jpg .tif …) and
       .npy arrays are read directly. Anything else — DICOM, NIfTI, WFDB — needs a
       four-line loader and the record kind; there is an example in the repository.

    
label

    
The training label: a class index or a name. For multi-label tasks, the positive classes
       separated by semicolons, like cardiomegaly;effusion.

    
patient_id

    
The identifier of the person. Every record of one person must carry the same
       value, across studies and years. An accession number, a file name or a lesion id is not a
       patient id. This is the one rule nothing downstream can check: a wrong key produces
       numbers that look perfectly plausible and mean nothing.

    
What counts as one record

    
A patient's risk is the maximum over their records, so hundreds of slices per
       patient saturate it and the ranking stops discriminating. For MRI or CT use one series per
       record; three to twenty records per patient is the comfortable range.

    
Without patient identifiers

    
The screen still runs, per record rather than per patient — and it says so everywhere,
       because a patient with several records is then not scored as one person.
